        Off our list

        • Easy-open button batteries
        • Tiny loose magnets
        • Knockoff characters

        A gift shouldn't come with a trip to urgent care. We skip toys that let small hands reach button batteries, sets with tiny loose magnets, and lookalikes of the characters kids love. Everything here is a name-brand toy with the maker's age guidance on the box.

        Free guides

        Read this before you wrap.

        Four short guides for the grown-ups. All free, and all worth more than anything in your cart.

        • Guide 1 · By age

          What each age actually plays with

          • 1 to 3: cause and effect. Buttons that make a sound, big bricks to stack and knock down, anything they can push.
          • 4 to 6: pretend play and "I did it myself." Simple builds, stories they can start on their own, a first camera.
          • 7 to 9: rules, collections and projects with steps. Card games, LEGO sets, coding and circuit kits.
          • 10 to 12: real skills and whatever their friends are into. Bigger builds and gadgets with grown-up features.
          Shop by age

        • Guide 2 · Safety

          Check the box, not just the bow

          • The age on the box is mostly about safety, not smarts. Under 3? Skip anything with small parts.
          • A quick test: if a piece fits through a toilet paper tube, it's too small for a child under 3.
          • Button batteries should sit behind a door that needs a screwdriver. Keep spare batteries up high.
          • Big kids' sets have small pieces. Store them where a younger sibling can't reach.
          See picks for 2-year-olds

        • Guide 3 · Tech gifts

          Set it up before you wrap it

          • Check what's not in the box. Toniebox Lite needs a USB-C cable, Botley needs AAA batteries and Snap Circuits needs AA.
          • Charge everything the night before, and download apps and updates on your own Wi-Fi.
          • Set parental controls before the box is opened: time limits, content filters and who can be contacted.
          • Take toys out of "try me" mode. Tickle Me Elmo has a wire on the bottom to pull out first.
          See the Screen-Free Kit

        • Guide 4 · Fewer, better

          Fewer gifts, more play

          • Try the four-gift rule: something they want, something they need, something to wear and something to read.
          • One gift that fits their age beats five that don't. Use the age tags to check.
          • Rotate toys. Put half away and swap them every few weeks, and old toys feel new again.
          • Relatives asking what to get? Send them your list so nobody doubles up.

        Who is this site for?

        Grown-ups: parents, grandparents, aunts, uncles and family friends shopping for kids 1 to 12. It's written for adults, not for kids.

        How do you choose the picks?

        We start from this season's hot-toy lists and the big holiday gift guides, then keep the picks with a tech or STEM angle that fit the age on the box and don't need surprise extras on the day. We don't test toys ourselves, so each pick says why it's popular and what the maker recommends. Nobody pays to be on this list.

        What do the ages mean?

        Each pick shows the maker's recommended age. Tap a number to see everything that fits that age. When in doubt, follow the box, because age labels are about safety as much as skill.
